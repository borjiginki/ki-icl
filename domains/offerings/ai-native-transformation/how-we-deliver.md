# How we deliver

## The commercial idea: you buy evidence, not a program

Transformation programmes fail commercially the same way they fail technically — by front-loading commitment before evidence exists. We sell the arc the opposite way.

Each module is a **self-contained trade**: its own scope, its own deliverables, its own acceptance. It ends at a **gate** where the evidence is on the table and the client decides — **continue, pause, or stop.** The next module's scope and price are fixed *at that gate*, when both sides know what they are pricing. Nothing is priced from afar, and stopping is a legitimate outcome rather than a failure.

Say the hinge before the module list. If a room hears "module 1, module 2…" before it hears *"you buy evidence, not a program"*, we have read out a rate card.

## The four modules

### 1 — Discover ("you just talk")

Entry, measured in days. A short qualification conversation first, to establish the three non-negotiables; if they are absent we say so and stop. Then the client walks us through one painful process with their real files on the table, and we co-create the working loop live — trigger, the context the agent needs, the action on their system of record, the human review, the signal captured back — and show a quick win in the same session.

**They leave with:** the loop sketch, a rough architecture and mock-up, and a lighthouse shortlist scored honestly.
**Gate:** a loop is drawn and a win was shown in the room. The lighthouse decision is now concrete.

### 2 — Lighthouse

One chosen loop taken to production in roughly six weeks — and only that loop, because one working end-to-end loop earns more trust than ten half-built ones. Scope and architecture are written down before building starts, and the loop runs on infrastructure the client already owns wherever that passes our foundation-fit check.

Before go-live: an eval baseline defining what "good" means, and a compliance pass — GDPR, ISO 27001, EU AI Act — before anyone touches real data. The agent starts in shadow mode and graduates on outcome evidence only.

**Gate:** the loop runs closed, quality is trending up, and the human owner of the process has signed off. No sign-off, no pass.

### 3 — Champion enablement (the waves)

Scale is where most programmes quietly re-hire the consultants. This module sells the opposite: capability that stays.

We train a cell of five to seven of the client's own people through a curriculum-backed arc — their own agent setup, their own reusable skills, their first loop designs — coached on real work. Then the cell, not us, builds roughly ten processes as loops in a two-week sprint; about half prove team-reusable and get promoted into the shared registry, and the rest legitimately stay personal. We shift from building to coaching, and the people the first wave trained seed the next.

Each wave is its own fixed-price module, so the engagement scales with the client's cell rather than with our headcount.

**Gate:** at least one non-engineer has shipped a reusable agent, and about half the cell's loops earned promotion — the moment the transformation becomes something the organization does itself.

### 4 — Foundation

The wiring is the work: enterprise connections, source-system access, productionizing — the unglamorous bulk of the effort. Loops consolidate onto one governed Foundation, with model serving kept swappable, a tool registry, a skill library, and the shared context layer with write-back.

Governance is built in from the start: every agent under a defined identity with one responsible person behind it, every action auditable, autonomy earned per process on evidence. The nightly learning loop goes live, so every use throws off a signal and the layer improves while they sleep.

It ends with a formal handover — runbooks, named owners, on-call — plus an optional standing improvement cadence, re-bookable as its own module.

**Gate, and the finish line:** the system improves week over week without a lessons-learned meeting, and the client owns the operating layer.

## What each side brings

**The client, from day one:** a named owner, a named high-volume process, and leadership air-cover. From the enablement waves onward: the builder cell, and the access our engineers need.

**Us:** every module fixed-price and separately accepted against named deliverables, with milestone-based payment inside the larger modules, and the next module scoped and priced at the previous gate. Where a client prefers to fix the total first, the same mechanics run as **design-to-budget**: the programme budget is set and its split across modules is sharpened during discovery.

**On numbers:** "fixed price per module, priced at the previous gate" is the complete answer at this altitude. Concrete figures are a function of the client's processes and landscape and belong in the individual offer document.
