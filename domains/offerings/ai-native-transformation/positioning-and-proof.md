# Positioning and proof

## The pain, and why now

The buyer's situation, in the pitch's own terms: intelligence is rented and identical for everyone, so impact is decided by context — the one factor they control. Their best people are already delegating to agents, but single-player: everything learned leaves with the person. And the organizational response so far is a chatbot or copilot rollout — rungs one and two of a six-rung ladder, delivering a few percent while looking like an AI strategy.

The cost of waiting is not standing still. Knowledge keeps walking out the door, and every month a competitor's context layer compounds is a month the gap widens on its own.

## Where the offering is strongest

Value scales with **team heterogeneity, vendor rotation, and knowledge churn** — wherever consistency cannot come from individual expertise, the shared layer *is* the operating model.

The inverse defines our weakest fit: **stable, elite, long-tenured teams**, who have often already banked much of the gain from off-the-shelf copilots. We concede that quickly when we meet it, because conceding it fast buys credibility.

## Who actually buys

Observed across engagements, not aspirational:

- **The function owner** of an operations-heavy function with high-volume, structured, still-manual workflows. Buying trigger: measurable grind, AI pressure from above, and a named internal champion.
- **The leadership of a mid-sized organization** buying a whole-organization programme, with AI-native positioning as a strategic differentiator, bought top-down with a champion motor built in.

**Qualification is part of the positioning.** The three non-negotiables — named owner, named high-volume process, leadership air-cover — plus the lighthouse test — willing owner, gains not already banked, a measurable baseline — are the honest filter. Enthusiasm is not a segment.

## What we compete against

**1 — The chatbot or copilot rollout**, which is what most companies currently call their AI strategy. Rungs one and two of the ladder: single-player, flat tools. We sell the jump to multiplayer, and the ask that proves the difference is a working closed loop, not a license count.

**2 — The platform vendors.** Think three rings: rent the substrate, buy the pipe. The platforms ship access, SSO, and compliance well. They do not ship the write-back context layer or the learning loop today — that is our software edge, and we label it honestly as commoditizable. They will never ship the refounding, because it does not exist until it is done with the client's people on their processes.

**3 — Classic consulting.** Their deliverable peaks on handover day and decays. Ours starts as a working agent on day one and improves nightly. We do not hand over recommendations; we hand over an operating layer the client owns, plus people who can extend it.

## What we can point to

- **We run on it ourselves.** KI group, around fifty people, works from its own shared context layer — offers, decks and analyses drafted by agents from shared context, with senior review confirming the quality beats individual work. Pair this with the scale bridge: the story reliably triggers "how does this scale to five thousand people?"
- **At enterprise scale:** a discovery process at a multi-thousand-person energy company went from eight to twelve weeks down to **two weeks**, with output consistent regardless of who runs it — a number quoted by that organization's own people.
- **Reference builds** running in energy and automotive, with a first full three-phase delivery of this offering live at an automotive client since mid-2026.
