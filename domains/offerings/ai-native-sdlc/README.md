## The reframe that opens every conversation

Most organizations ask *"where can we add AI to our SDLC?"* — and bolt a copilot onto the delivery process they already have. That gets everyone the same few percent.

The real question is: **how would software delivery work if agents did the first draft of everything** — specs, code, tests, docs?

Every process in the delivery org was designed around two actors: humans and software. AI-native means redesigning each one around **three** — humans, software, and agents. That is a refounding, not an add-on.

## Why engineering is a strong entry

The artifacts are already text, already versioned, already in systems with APIs — repos, trackers, wikis, pipelines. The distance between "how we work" and "something an agent can act on" is shorter here than in most functions, which is why engineering often produces the first credible loop in an organization.

The catch, and it is the load-bearing insight of this cut: **most existing engineering documentation was written for the old world.** Some is irrelevant now, and some is actively misleading to an agent — and garbage gets amplified, not absorbed. The job is not to digitize the wiki. It is to build a different kind of memory.

## What we build

One governed layer over the systems that stay the systems of record — repos, tracker, wiki, pipelines. It holds what the organization knows, how it actually works, and who may do what. The people and their agents read from it, do the job, and **write the result back**. Read-only is a mirror; read-and-write is a memory.

On top of it, loops: the delivery loop redesigned around three actors, then the next one, then the next — each cheaper than the last, because each leaves reusable assets on the layer.
